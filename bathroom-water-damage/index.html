Bathroom Water Damage: What Jacksonville Homeowners Should Know

Bathrooms are built to get wet on the surface, not underneath it. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. Our Jacksonville team finds where the water is getting in, measures how far it has gone, and dries or removes what is wet.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

Signs to Look For

  • Edges of the vinyl floor peeling up, or dark discoloration showing through it
  • The bottom panel inside the vanity has swelled, stained or bowed
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity
  • A toilet that rocks slightly, or dark staining around its base
  • The room keeps a damp, musty smell even right after a thorough cleaning
  • Directly under the bathroom, a ceiling with a brown stain or a sag
Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

The first several weeks

The Subfloor Never Dries

Between showers, the wood below the tile has no time to dry out. Within the wall cavity, and across the bottom of the floor, mold begins.

Months one to three

Signs Show Up

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

The Structure Suffers

Joists can begin to rot, and the subfloor near the toilet or tub becomes soft. Drying alone no longer fixes it, because carpentry and new tile are now part of the repair.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Leaking Shower Pan

A cracked pan or liner lets water out under the shower floor every time it is used. Up top everything looks fine, yet the framing underneath never dries.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Trapped Steam

Steam lingers after each shower when the exhaust fan is weak or absent. It condenses on the ceiling and walls, encourages mold, and keeps already wet materials from drying.

Rot Under the Toilet

A failed wax ring leaks a little with every flush. The toilet eventually rocks because this category 3 water has rotted the subfloor at the flange.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Using dehumidifiers and air movers, we dry the wall framing, the subfloor and the ceiling cavity underneath. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

New subfloor and backer board go in, and we can coordinate the tile, toilet seal, caulk and ceiling work, meaning the path of the leak gets closed and not just hidden.
